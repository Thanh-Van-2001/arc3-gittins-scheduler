# Correcting an Error in Competition Submission Limits  (id 705405, votes None, by María Cruz)

Hi everyone,

We want to sincerely apologize for a recent oversight regarding the daily submission limits for this competition, and share how we are addressing it.

#### What happened:
Our intended setting for this competition had been one submission per day. However, on May 27, a miscommunication over a mismatch between our settings and the written rules caused us to mistakenly change the competition settings to allow 5 submissions per day.

#### The Impact:
During this 13-day window, the system allowed up to 5 submissions per day. Understandably, several teams utilized these available submissions to test and improve their models. We want to be entirely clear that we do not view this as cheating or breaking the rules; teams were simply operating within the limits our system presented to them.

#### Going forward:
To get back to the intended course, we are taking the following steps:
- **Restoring the Cap:** Yesterday, June 8, we restored the submission limit back to one submission per day.
- **Invalidating Surplus Submissions:** We are working now to invalidate any additional submissions teams made after their first successful submission for each day during that period. Because there are still 5 months remaining in the competition, this brief window of elevated submission allowance does not confer a material unfair advantage to teams, and resetting the counts now ensures all teams compete on equal footing for the remainder of the competition.

We understand that errors such as this are frustrating and disruptive. We very much appreciate your patience and continued participation in the Kaggle community.

Sincerely, 

María and the Kaggle team.

--- Reki (votes {'canUpvote': True}): Hi María and the Kaggle team, thanks for the transparency. But I'd like to point out a problem with this fix.

Invalidating the surplus submissions doesn't actually remove the advantage they created. During those 13 days, teams who submitted up to 5 times per day ran 50-60 extra experiments and learned exactly which approach scored best and what their ceiling was. That knowledge can't be erased. After the cap was restored, they can simply re-submit their already-validated best solution once, and the score comes right back. So deleting the records does not reset anyone to an equal footing - the real edge was the feedback/information from all those trials, not the submission count itself.

Meanwhile, the teams actually penalized by this rollback are the ones who played it safe or submitted less - they lose positions through no fault of their own, while the teams who exploited the extra attempts keep the insight.

A fairer option: rather than deleting valid submissions that everyone equally had the right to make, grant the same extra-submission budget to teams who joined the competition before the bug appeared but didn't get to benefit from it. That way everyone gets comparable opportunity to explore, instead of only some teams keeping the knowledge while others are pushed down the leaderboard. At minimum, please acknowledge this was a platform bug and avoid arbitrarily voiding submissions that were made entirely within the limits the system presented.

Thanks for considering this.
