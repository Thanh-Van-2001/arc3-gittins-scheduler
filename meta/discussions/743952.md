    --- Tony Li (votes {'totalVotes': 2, 'canUpvote': True, 'totalUpvotes': 2}): About : Why not automatically select the two submissions with the highest hidden/private scores?

The core concern is that this would remove most of the risk from final submission selection and significantly change competitors’ incentives.

1. Public LB would become less meaningful.
    Teams could overfit the public leaderboard even much more aggressively, because they would no longer need to decide whether a high-LB submission was actually robust. If any submitted variant happened to score well privately, Kaggle would automatically choose it.

2. It would encourage maximum submission diversity and quota usage.
    There would be almost no downside to submitting every seed, model, post-processing variant, or completely different approach. Every submission becomes another lottery ticket against the hidden test set.

So while automatic private selection might reduce “bad final-pick luck,” it could simultaneously increase “many-lottery-ticket luck.”

The current system forces teams to make a real judgment about generalization: which submissions they actually trust, which gains may be overfit, and how much diversity to keep. 

Removing that decision could increase randomness and reward submission quota more heavily rather than making the competition fairer.
